The old PH8 connector is removed; all three PH6 sensor pinouts remain unchanged.
Host GPIO16-21 must be inputs without pulls. See backplane-encoder-bus.md.

CONTROLLER DECISION — 7 NOVEMBER 2026

Use one bare RP2350B for XYZ, rotation, heater and vacuum. Remove the STM32.
The backplane master sends commands and coordinates the machine; the card
owns encoder feedback and time-sensitive actuator control. Keeping those
loops local avoids depending on shared-bus traffic or host scheduling.
The former arrangement mainly preserved the two upstream firmware projects.
No required electrical interface needs a second MCU.

SILICON REVISION
Fit RP2350B A4, specified by the schematic MPN and BOM. A4 includes the fix
for erratum 9 (Bank 0 input leakage). This matters to weak pull-ups and the
USB VBUS sense divider; early A2 silicon is not an approved substitution.

RESOURCE CHECK
The current allocation uses 42 of 48 GPIOs, leaving GPIO42..47 spare.
Dedicated USB, SWD, reset and QSPI pins are outside that GPIO count.

  XYZ winding drive: 12 hardware PWM outputs, GPIO4..15, slices 2..7.
  Rotation drive:     4 hardware PWM outputs, GPIO32..35, slices 8 and 9.
  Heater:            1 hardware PWM output, GPIO36, slice 10A.
  Vacuum:            1 hardware PWM output, GPIO38, slice 11A.
  Auxiliary tools:   2 existing PIO PWM outputs, GPIO16/17.
  XYZ encoders:      SPI0, three separate chip-select signals.
  Rotation encoder:  SPI1, separate chip-select signal.
  Backplane UART:    UART0, GPIO28 TX and GPIO29 RX.
  Backplane I2C0:    RP2350 I2C1, GPIO30 SDA and GPIO31 SCL.
  Analogue inputs:   GPIO40/ADC0 temperature; GPIO41/ADC1 12 V sensing.

Both outputs within a PWM slice share a frequency. The allocation keeps
20 kHz XYZ drive, proposed 25 kHz rotation/vacuum, and proposed 1 kHz heater
in distinct slices. The two optional 8 kHz tool outputs remain PIO.
See controller-pin-allocation.csv for every GPIO and physical package pin.

INTERFACES AND POWER
The DIN96 logical pin map is unchanged. No extra 3.3 V contact is introduced.
Power comes from backplane 12 V and 5 V, with local 5 V-to-3.3 V conversion.
The core LC network is mandatory RP2350 support. The XYZ splitter uses existing backplane SPI0 with three original-pinout PH6 ports.
The rotation 1x6 encoder pin order stays. USB-C is a service connection;
USB VBUS is sensed, not tied to backplane 5 V.

Preserve the UART motion protocol and I2C tool register protocol in firmware.
They can terminate on the same MCU while retaining the existing contacts.
Using local I2C1 does not rename or change the physical backplane I2C0 bus.

WHY THIS HELPS, AND THE TRADEOFF
Removing the STM32 removes its crystal, reset/boot support and duplicate
programming interfaces. It leaves one firmware image and one shared view
of motion/tool state. RP2350B's 10 x 10 mm package is larger than the A
variant; savings come mainly from deleting supporting circuitry. The fixed
DIN connector still sets a lower bound on board width.

RP2350B has more pins, not more processing power than RP2350A. The remaining
question is worst-case execution time, not peripheral availability. Three
six-byte XYZ SPI reads take at least 18 us at 8 MHz. A six-byte rotation read
takes at least 48 us at 1 MHz. These are calculated wire times, excluding
software overhead and computation, not measured loop times. Do not simply
add every rotation read to every XYZ iteration. Use explicit task rates
and/or overlapping SPI DMA after measuring actual control requirements.

FIRMWARE WORK REQUIRED BEFORE THIS HARDWARE CAN BE RUN
1. Use an RP2350B target supporting GPIO32..47 and ADC GPIO40..47.
2. Give encoder buses, PWM and ADC access clear task ownership. Use bounded
   command queues and consistent updates of state shared between cores.
3. Replace blocking homing/calibration in active control paths with bounded
   state machines. Quiesce loads before flash writes, or implement and verify
   a suitable RAM-resident control scheme that survives flash programming.
4. Replace the motion loop's dt <=100 us clamp with real elapsed time,
   deadline detection and a defined stop/fault policy. Review the inherited
   JOINT_READY_OVERRIDE before running any motion.
5. Keep logs and host callbacks out of critical control work. Measure worst
   case timing under USB/UART/I2C traffic, homing, encoder faults and all
   requested loads. CPU speed alone does not establish timing margin.
6. Feed the watchdog only while control and communications tasks remain
   healthy. Define command-loss behavior and explicit recovery/rearming.
   Reset must leave motor enables and heater/vacuum controls off.
7. Retain the independent thermal cutoff in the heater supply. A second
   general-purpose MCU is not automatically a safety controller. A stopped
   CPU can leave hardware PWM running until reset.
8. Implement GPIO1 VBUS detection and self-powered USB behavior.
