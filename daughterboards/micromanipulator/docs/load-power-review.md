# Load supplies and fuses

The existing backplane provides the 12 V motor and 5 V tool rails. No connector contact or rail assignment changes. Fuses provide secondary branch protection; the host supplies must be current-limited and their available fault current must remain within the fuse interrupt rating.

| Reference | Exact part | Supplier | Rating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1, 5 V load branch | Littelfuse 0466004.NRHF | [JLCPCB C187596](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/Littelfuse-0466004NRHF/C187596) | 4 A, 32 V AC/DC |
| F2, 12 V XYZ branch | Littelfuse 0466004.NRHF | [JLCPCB C187596](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/Littelfuse-0466004NRHF/C187596) | 4 A, 32 V AC/DC |

Both use the [Littelfuse 466 series specification](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/fuse-466-datasheet?assetguid=dbe9bcd7-6072-4adf-bf5b-d33e52a6b90f). Catalog identity was checked on 2026-11-07; inventory is not reserved. The old 125 V labels were generic placeholders. Both have a 50 A interrupt rating at rated voltage. Both use the manufacturer's nominal melting integral of 1.024 A-squared-seconds; differing distributor values are not used. These common fuses do not provide individual current limiting or guaranteed protection for every 2 A harness contact.

## Current envelope

The [source micromanipulator documentation](https://docs.hackerfab.org/home/fab-toolkit/nanopositioner/micromanipulator#pcb-2) specifies 12 V, 0.4 A, 30 ohm XYZ windings. Energizing two windings on each of three axes gives 2.4 A nominal; 12.6 V across six nominal 30 ohm windings gives 2.52 A before bridge loss. This is the design load basis, not a guaranteed bound on unknown motor resistance tolerance.

The fuse manufacturer's standard continuous-current factor is 0.75, with an additional temperature factor. Using a conservative 0.85 screening factor at 50 C gives 2.55 A for either fuse. The host supply imposes a lower limit on the 5 V loads. The former 3 A F2 would give only 1.91 A. At 70 C the manufacturer's explicit 0.80 factor reduces F2 to 2.4 A; continuous operation of all axes at high rail voltage is outside this screen. The card's provisional local-ambient target remains 50 C.

The original [heater PCB](https://github.com/hacker-fab/MicroManipulatorStepper/blob/fa9a1f8e6d5365c6d5d5f31054d4f2c95aff8f6a/electronics/heater_pcb/heater_pcb.kicad_pcb) specifies 35 um copper on both heating layers. Its heat net is one connected series chain between J2 pins 1 and 2: 876 trace segments and one layer-transfer via, with no parallel branches in the endpoint graph. Summing the trace lengths and widths gives 1.166 m of copper and approximately 3.742 ohms using room-temperature copper resistivity of 0.01724 ohm-mm-squared/m. The resulting cold estimates are 1.34 A / 6.68 W at 5 V and 1.40 A at 5.25 V. This excludes via/contact resistance, etch tolerance and self-heating; it is an estimate from the source geometry, not a measured heater specification. It rules out the previous 1.2 A combined tool allowance.

The **provisional combined continuous 5 V tool allowance is at most 2 A**, including heater, pump and both rotation windings, and is conditional on the remaining host budget below. Gate-driver and logic consumption is upstream of F1 and is accounted for separately. With the source heater at 5.25 V, at most approximately 0.6 A remains for rotation and vacuum together under that 2 A allocation. Individual DRV8837C outputs remain limited by their own 1 A device rating and thermal conditions; PH contacts remain subject to JST's 2 A conditions and the actual mating contacts/wire. Peak motor-start/stall current and PWM RMS current must also fit the drivers, contacts and fuse time-current curves. A 4 A fuse is not a 4 A continuous tool rating or an active current limiter.

The source [rotation board](https://github.com/hacker-fab/MicroManipulatorStepper/blob/fa9a1f8e6d5365c6d5d5f31054d4f2c95aff8f6a/electronics/rot_platform_pcb/rot_platform_pcb.kicad_sch) powers the heater, pump and two TPM8837C modules from 5 V, using IRLZ44N low-side switches. Its [firmware configuration](https://github.com/hacker-fab/MicroManipulatorStepper/blob/fa9a1f8e6d5365c6d5d5f31054d4f2c95aff8f6a/firmware/rotation-stage-controller/src/config.h) specifies 50 rotation steps and PWM settings, but no winding resistance or current. The [BOM](https://github.com/hacker-fab/MicroManipulatorStepper/blob/fa9a1f8e6d5365c6d5d5f31054d4f2c95aff8f6a/documentation/bom/bom.md) does not identify the rotation motor or pump. The main checkout and hardware-related branches were checked; no load part number or stall-current specification was found. Compatibility with unspecified loads is therefore not asserted. The host's 5 V buck feeds U6 (TPS2116) VIN1 pin 3; its VOUT pins 2 and 7 feed the +5V net at every DIN slot. The [TPS2116](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2116.pdf) is rated for 2.5 A maximum continuous current. This shared path, rather than the buck's 5 A headline rating, limits the installed system. Require I_tools + I_card_logic_and_gates + I_host + I_other_cards < 2.5 A, with thermal and transient margin. The card logic allocation is 0.25 A; allowing 2 A for its tools leaves less than 0.25 A for the host, gate driver and other cards. If those need more, reduce the tool allocation. Startup/stall pulses also require review; the fuse does not protect the mux against every overload. The earlier proposed 3 A tool budget is withdrawn. The fixed backplane remains unchanged, and full simultaneous operation of unspecified loads is not qualified.

## Footprint and copper

The project-local `FabNano:Fuse_Littelfuse_0466_1206` uses the manufacturer's reflow example: two 1.143 by 2.032 mm lands, 1.524 mm inner gap and 3.810 mm overall span. Its fabrication body is 3.175 by 1.524 mm. The nominal KiCad 1206 model is retained and explicitly approximate; manufacturer height is 0.584 +/-0.076 mm.

Both fuses' short shared input and output tracks are widened to 1.2 mm. F1 uses three parallel vias on each side; F2's output also uses three, each with a 0.30 mm drill and 0.80 mm land. The per-axis capacitor/driver connections retain their direct front-copper routing. Net membership and all non-fuse pads are unchanged. The main motor distribution is a broad In1 region down the left side and across the axis drivers; it does not use the thin ADC sense branch to deliver motor power.

At 35 um outer copper, the shared 1.2 mm section has 0.042 mm-squared cross-section. With a conservative 20 um barrel-plating assumption, three 0.30 mm drills provide about 0.057 mm-squared total barrel cross-section. These dimensions support the short feed but are not a substitute for thermal measurement. Internal-plane copper is nominally 15.2 um. Inspect actual fabricated copper and validate temperature with all axes energized, pump starting and the heater active.

## Inrush and bring-up

The XYZ rail has three 220 uF reservoirs. At 12.6 V they store about 52 mJ; the 5 V load rail's 220 uF reservoir stores about 3 mJ at 5.25 V. Startup pulse shape depends on host current limiting, wiring resistance and capacitor ESR, so energy alone does not prove fuse survival. Power down before inserting/removing the card. Check startup current, motor stall behavior, voltage overshoot and temperatures with the actual harnesses before commissioning. These are hardware validation steps; no bench measurements are claimed here.
